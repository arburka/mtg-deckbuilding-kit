# The Problem with Deckbuilding Templates | The Command Zone 659

**Channel:** The Command Zone (Josh Lee Kwai, Rachel Weeks) | **Published:** Feb 26, 2025 | **Length:** 1:46:38

**Premise:** A follow-up to episode 658's "New Era" deckbuilding template. The template is a solid starting point, but nearly every deck should deviate from it. Each deviation should have a reason you can state ("I run less X because...").

## The baseline template

| Category | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Ramp | 10 |
| Card advantage | 12 |
| Targeted disruption (1-for-1) | 12 |
| Mass disruption | 6 |
| Lands | 38 |
| Plan cards | \\~30 (overlaps other categories) |

The totals add up to more than 100 because cards overlap categories.

## Recommended process

1.  Sort every card into a category bucket (they use Archidekt). If a card fits no bucket, ask why it's in the deck.
2.  Goldfish 50–100 times. Their playtest episode is #596.
3.  Adjust based on what you observe. Common reasons to deviate are your meta, playgroup preferences, and mechanics (the episode focuses on mechanics).

## Exceptions by category

**Ramp (baseline 10)**

  - *Run less:* low curve; a cheap (2-mana) commander you want to cast on curve; a commander that ramps; alternate cost cheating (cost reducers, cascade, reanimation)
  - *Run more:* high curve; multi-step, mana-hungry plans; a 4+ mana commander you want out early; landfall or artifact synergy (ramp doubles as plan); a mana sink on the commander; heavy card draw

**Card advantage (baseline 12)**

  - *Run less:* a commander that draws; lots of looting or graveyard access (count these as partial card advantage); a big-spell plan where quality beats quantity
  - *Run more:* low curve; spells-matter win conditions; lots of ramp; lower-power cards vs. a stronger playgroup (win by volume); multi-step plans like aristocrats or lands; card draw directly pays off your plan
  - Caution: going under is riskier than going over, since excess cards can be discarded

**Targeted disruption (baseline 12)** Includes tap, phasing, and land destruction effects.

  - *Run less:* hyper-aggressive decks; ways to reuse removal (blink, recursion, Torrential Gearhulk); weak removal colors (green, red vs. enchantments); heavier mass disruption; removal built into the commander
  - *Run more:* higher power level; heavy card draw; a compact or slow plan; holding up mana regularly; spells or enchantress synergy

**Mass disruption (baseline 6)** Not just board wipes. Also includes group tapping, mass bounce, graveyard hate, and similar effects.

  - *Run less:* aggressive, board-committed decks; colors weak at wraths
  - *Run more:* low board commitment or spells decks; expecting to be the archenemy; tight slots; protection or recursion that lets you wipe offensively

**Lands (baseline 38)**

  - *Run less:* average mana value about 2.5 or below; lots of low-cost cantrips; heavy fast mana (cEDH)
  - Not valid reasons to cut lands: a lax mulligan policy, frequent flooding (that's a card advantage problem), or needing more spell slots
  - Reduce one land at a time, never in big cuts
  - *Run more:* high curve; less ramp; lots of looting (loot lands away rather than into them); mulliganing for a specific piece; landfall (~45)
  - Key goldfish check: are you hitting land drops 4–5 consistently? If not, add lands or card draw.

## Using math to find "the magic number"

They used Salubrious Snail's calculators and Archidekt's probability stats.

| Deck | Question | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sedisi (40 lands, 12 two-mana ramp) | 3 lands + 1 two-mana ramp in opening hand | 31% in the opening hand, 53% by the 1st mulligan, 68% by the 2nd. With 15 ramp: 37% / 60% / 75% |
| Rakdos (15 free-activation pingers) | Draw at least one by turn 2 / turn 3 | 78.7% / 82.3%; deemed acceptable |

## Troubleshooting common complaints

  - **Mana screwed despite running 38 lands:** likely needs more card advantage, or more lands if the deck plays extra land drops
  - **Always discarding to hand size:** curve too high, or not enough ramp
  - **Mulliganing 3–4 times for a playable hand:** curve too high; not enough proactive two-drops (removal doesn't count)
  - **Drawing pieces out of order:** the deck is trying to do too much, or lacks card selection

**Most common beginner mistakes:**

  - Trying to do too many things
  - Curve too high, with weak early plays

**Default fix when unsure:** confirm you have 38 lands, then add card draw. They compare card draw to "drink more water." Changes are cheap to test digitally and easy to revert.
