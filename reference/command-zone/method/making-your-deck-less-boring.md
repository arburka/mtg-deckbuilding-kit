## Purpose / Overview

  - Covers why decks that were exciting at first become repetitive after many plays, and how to keep them in rotation.
  - Catalogs the different ways a deck can become boring.
  - Offers a menu of independent fixes. The hosts say you don't need to apply all or any of them.
  - Puts fun, decision-making, and deck longevity ahead of raw power or consistency. Several fixes deliberately make a deck slightly weaker.
  - Closes with each host's current most interesting deck to play.

## Key Takeaways

| Lever | What it means |

| Cut tutors | Frees a slot and removes "always find the same card" games |
| Eliminate power outliers | Remove haymakers (e.g., Cyclonic Rift, Skullclamp) so tutoring and drawing become real choices |
| Add card draw | More cards in hand means more decisions and more game actions |

## 0:08 – Intro

  - Hosts introduce themselves. The topic is steps to make a deck more interesting, give it more choices, and keep it in your collection longer.

## 0:40 – How to Make Your Deck Less Boring

  - Common pattern: a premise deck is exciting the first several times, then becomes predictable and gets shelved.
  - Compared to always ordering the same dish at a restaurant: good the first 10 times, then less so.

## 1:42 – There Are a Lot of Ways to Be Boring

| Type of boring | Description and examples |
| --- | --- |
| One-trick pony | Commander telegraphs the plan (Prossh cited). Opponents see it coming, which also makes the deck less effective. Same finisher every game (Craterhoof Behemoth). |
| Linear gameplay | Scripted first five or six turns: turn 2 setup, turn 3 commander, turn 4 payoff. Heavy redundancy. |
| Decks that look alike | Early builders repeat the same cards across decks. Jimmy had three mono-red decks. |
| Doesn't do much | Short turns ("cast, equip, attack, pass") while others do more interesting things. |
| Too easy to disrupt | Glass-cannon decks reliant on one piece. Jimmy's Animar deck stalled when Animar was removed. |
| Card crossover across decks | Rachel owned eight copies of Teferi's Protection in eight decks and tired of casting it. |

## 6:11 – How Do You Fight Boredom?

  - **Take lots of game actions:** use activated and channel abilities, and try to spend all your mana every turn.
  - **Make more decisions:** having several options on turn 3 that lead to different outcomes is what makes Commander engaging, versus the deck playing itself.
  - **Stay involved on opponents' turns:** keep some interaction or abilities available, and re-plan as the board changes even when tapped out.
  - **Play varied cards and strategies:** swap a strong card for a slightly weaker one with interesting synergy, or try turning a deck's aggression up.
  - **Framing:** these changes favor fun and longevity, not optimization. Power and consistency work against intrigue.
  - Analogies: moving from Mozart to Bach and Beethoven on piano, and cross-training (throw a javelin, not just hurdles).

## 12:40 – Cut Tutors

  - Tutors can add toolbox decisions and enable secret-commander builds. In higher-power pods they may be required.
  - They can also cause repetitive play when you always fetch the same card (e.g., an equipment tutor always finding Skullclamp).
  - Test: do tutors give you more decisions, or just the same card every game?
  - Upside of cutting them: you have to work with what you draw, and it opens a flex slot (likened to running one fewer land).

## 15:46 – Eliminate Power Outliers

  - If you keep tutors, remove the card it's always correct to fetch so each tutor becomes a real choice.
  - Flatten the deck's power curve. Candidates named: Cyclonic Rift, Skullclamp, possibly Sol Ring.
  - Rachel replaced Open the Armory with a Kellan creature card that carries a similar tutor effect. She now often just casts the creature.
  - In graveyard decks, cutting Living Death or Rise of the Dark Realms forces new routes to victory (combat, aristocrats).
  - Downgrade a finisher instead of cutting it: Craterhoof Behemoth to Pathbreaker Ibex.
  - Deeper dive: a video from another creator on tutoring and power outliers. Rachel planned to link it, but the name is unclear in captions.

## 18:37 – Add Card Draw

  - Standard Command Zone advice: if a deck isn't working, add card draw.
  - More cards means more choices, more game actions, and more ways to solve the board.
  - Works especially well after cutting power outliers, since you're no longer digging for one specific card.
  - Running out of cards in hand is cited as a major source of boredom.

## 20:31 – Reduce Redundancy

  - Distinguishes this from commander backup (cards that replicate your commander), which they still recommend.
  - Problem: newer sets print so many similar effects (about 12 two-mana aristocrat drainers, many blue looters) that decks always win the same way.
  - Examples:

      - Rachel's angel deck could swap some aristocrat pieces for a Voltron option such as Hatred, instead of always winning with Mirkwood Bats loops.
      - A friend's red-green ramp deck follows a fixed pattern: 2-drop mana creature, turn-3 land ramp, turn-4 six-drop threat. One fix is rotating half the six-drop threats.
  - They call this the most controversial point: it makes the deck less consistent so the pilot gets more decisions and occasional surprise moments.

## 30:13 – Add More Decision Points

  - **Activated abilities:** create moments to choose during other players' turns (e.g., cracking a Clue).
  - **Modal cards:**

| Instead of | Try | Added flexibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reclamation Sage | Cankerbloom | Remove a permanent now, or hold it and proliferate later, at instant speed |
| Swords to Plowshares | Thraben Charm (or play both) | Exile graveyards, destroy a creature, or destroy an enchantment |

  - **Multiple routes to victory:** a main theme plus a subtheme (e.g., aristocrat payoffs and anthem effects that both care about going wide). Pick a path based on the table, like a roguelike or choose-your-own-adventure.

      - Offset the consistency loss with more card draw or tutors.
      - Example: a custom vintage cube at PAX Unplugged that used conspiracies to support off-the-beaten-path strategies.
  - Rachel avoids instant-win buttons (Craterhoof Behemoth, Torment of Hailfire, Exsanguinate) and prefers working out lethal from the board.
  - Deeper dive: the Command Zone deckbuilding template, recommended as the starting point for first builds.

## 37:04 – Add Variance

  - Rachel's Atla Palani deck had become routine: ramp on turn 2, Atla on turn 3, eggs on turns 4 and 5, then win.
  - Fix, from an early Command Zone episode: keep about 30 possible threats, randomly shuffle 12 into the deck each time. She also removed every effect that searches the library so egg results stayed unknown.
  - This extended the deck's life. Its last game was a pod of four Atla Palani decks.
  - Framing: decks are long-term relationships, not marriages. Evolving or retiring a deck can be better than letting it sit unplayed. Compared to athletes moving into broadcasting or coaching.
  - Prized or heavily upgraded ("blinged") decks often don't need changes because their owners already love playing them.

## 41:44 – Dump Your Commander

  - Sometimes the commander is the repetitive part. Prosper and Feather were cited, and Feather was named as a deck people often build and then disassemble.
  - Options:

      - Remove the commander temporarily. You can always bring it back.
      - Swap to another legend already in the 99 (Rachel's example used two legends from one deck; their names are unclear in captions).
      - Randomize: shuffle several commanders before each game. A friend's mono-red cast-from-exile deck rolls a die among six commanders, which changes mulligans, tutoring, and lines.
      - Playing a different commander exposes gaps the usual commander was covering (e.g., self-mill).
  - Treat decks as living documents. A deck was described as a bonsai tree to keep pruning.
  - Cut a color (three colors to two) to promote a two-color legend already in the deck. This frees utility land slots and forces digging deeper in the remaining colors.

## 47:45 – Remove Staples

| Staple | Replacement | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rampant Growth | Emergent Sequence | Ramp plus +1/+1 counter synergy |
| Generic removal or protection | Origin of Metalbending (Avatar set) | Combat trick, protection, or removal; adds a +1/+1 counter |

  - New sets keep printing modal versions of staples. This gives a deck its own identity.
  - **No-overlap challenge:** no two of your decks share a card except basic lands (concept credited to Alex Kessler and Ben Bateman). One Smothering Tithe and one Sol Ring across the whole collection. Rachel has about 32 decks, which would mean roughly 3,200 unique cards.
  - A looser version: apply the rule to only your next three builds.

## 50:04 – Add a Restriction

  - **Companions:** Keruga (Rachel has two Keruga decks) and Zirda.
  - **Budget:** Jimmy's favorite deck started as a budget build and evolved over time.
  - **Other restrictions:**

      - Pauper (commons and uncommons only)
      - Cards from a favorite draft era
      - A single block or setting
      - A single Universes Beyond property (Avatar, Spongebob, Lord of the Rings). Jimmy's Lord of the Rings deck uses only that set's cards and still competes.
      - Visual themes (e.g., every card shows gold)
  - **Creature-type subthemes:** lean into types you already have (humans, elves). Example: an artifact-graveyard Goblin Welder deck led by the red-black-green Ultimate Green Goblin, with Goblin Matron.
  - Tightly themed decks can still be powerful as long as the curve and basics are sound.

## 54:16 – Chase What You Like

  - Build around cards you're excited to draw, such as cards from the set you started with (e.g., Ice Age) or a pony-themed deck.
  - Chaos drafts and browsing old cards surface options that EDHREC and Archidekt recommendations miss.
  - Deeper dive: the Command Zone Scryfall episode with Jordan, covering search basics.

## 55:35 – Try Stuff

  - Making a deck about 2% worse costs nothing in casual Commander. Swap about 10 cards, play, keep what you liked.
  - Archidekt features mentioned:

      - Playtester for goldfishing
      - Deck snapshots (named save states you can restore or duplicate)
      - Pin board for saving cards while browsing other people's decks
  - Browsing random decks in your colors surfaces ideas you wouldn't find otherwise.

## Closing Guidance

  - Boredom usually comes from too few choices, too much consistency, or the same finisher every game.
  - Pick only the fixes that suit a given deck. Many trade a little power for more decisions and a longer life in your collection.
  - Treat decks as evolving projects: rebuild, randomize, restrict, or swap commanders before disassembling.
  - Follow-ups mentioned: the Scryfall episode, the earlier Atla Palani variance episode, the deckbuilding template, and a linked video on tutors and power outliers.
  - Viewers are invited to share in the comments how they fight deck boredom.
