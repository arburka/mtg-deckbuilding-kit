## 2. Purpose and Overview

  - Defines "mass disruption," the renamed version of the older "mass removal" category in the Command Zone deck-building template.
  - Explains why mass disruption is worth more than targeted disruption in four-player Commander.
  - Gives a recommended count and a breakdown of what each slot should cover.
  - Walks through nine types of mass disruption with example cards, plus edge cases where a card's role depends on the deck or commander.
  - Separates disruption (messes up opponents) from protection and plan cards (help your own strategy).

**Adjustments:**

  - Slower or control decks: play more mass disruption.
  - Faster decks: lean more on targeted disruption.
  - Spellslinger decks: point more slots at creatures.
  - Creature-heavy decks: point more slots at artifacts and enchantments.
  - Adjust for your playgroup's typical decks.

### A Complete Guide to Mass Disruption (1:13)

  - The category was renamed from mass removal to mass disruption.

### What Is Mass Disruption? (1:37)

  - One card that removes or deals with at least three other cards.
  - Now covers board wipes, multi-target removal, graveyard hate and mass value-based effects, not only "destroy everything."
  - The test: is the card there to interfere with what opponents are doing?

| Card role | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Interferes with opponents' plans | Mass disruption |
| Protects your own board or plan | Protection (falls under "plan") |
| Moves you toward winning | Plan |

  - Bounce, exile, destroy and counterspells can all count.
  - **Deeper dive:** the new deck-building template episode.

### Why Play Mass Disruption? (4:27)

| Targeted disruption | Mass disruption |
| --- | --- |
| Usually cheaper and instant-speed | Usually sorcery speed and costlier |
| Answers one threat (Swords to Plowshares, counterspells) | Answers many at once |
| Card disadvantage against the other two opponents | Card-efficient |
| Some give the opponent a replacement (Path to Exile) | Rarely gives anything back |
| Often comes with deal-making | Rarely negotiable |
| A speed bump | A reset |

  - One-sided mass disruption is especially strong; Cyclonic Rift and Flood of Tears-style bounce are the examples.
  - Mass disruption doesn't have to be a board wipe.

  - Six total, with the breakdown in Section 3.

  - Graveyard examples: Bojuka Bog, Scavenger Grounds and Time Spiral.
  - The segment ends with sponsor reads for Card Kingdom, Ultra PRO and Patreon. Noah Bose is the patron shout-out.

### Board Wipes (14:05)

  - Single spells that remove all or most of one permanent type. They can be symmetrical or one-sided, and work through bounce, exile, destroy, damage or -X/-X.

| Card | Type / note |
| --- | --- |
| Wrath of God | Classic destroy (white) |
| Toxic Deluge | \-X/-X (black) |
| Blasphemous Act | Damage-based (red) |
| Raise the Palisade | Bounce wipe, can be one-sided (blue) |
| Bane of Progress | Green non-creature wipe; green has few good creature wipes |
| Vandal Blast | One-sided artifact wipe; works as targeted or mass |
| Cyclonic Rift | Targeted or mass |
| Filter Out | 3 mana instant that bounces non-creature, non-land permanents (handles battles) |
| Farewell | Modal: artifacts, creatures, enchantments and/or graveyards |
| Austere Command | Modal, choose two |

  - Control decks want cheap wipes early and big threats late.
  - Proactive or aggressive decks benefit more from modal or one-sided wipes that miss their own setup.

### Multi-Target Removal (18:41)

  - X spells, cards with several targets, or "one per opponent" effects that can hit three or more cards.

| Card | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Decimate | Hits an artifact, creature, enchantment and land; needs all four targets |
| Dismantling Wave | 3 mana sorcery, one artifact or enchantment per opponent; cycling for 6 destroys all of them |
| March of Swirling Mist | Instant, phases out X creatures; can protect your board or remove theirs |

  - These behave much like one-sided wraths and can be used politically.

### Repeatable Removal (22:59)

  - Usually permanents that keep disrupting while also supporting the deck's plan.
  - The threat of activation alone discourages opponents from attacking or casting spells.

| Card | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Royal Assassin | Better with untappers |
| Goblin Bombardment | Doubles as a sacrifice outlet |
| Aura Shards | Triggered |
| Mayhem Devil | Pings whenever anyone sacrifices |
| Hullbreaker Horror | Bounces spells or non-land permanents |

  - This part of the episode ends with ads for HelloFresh, Square, Ridge and Archidekt.

### Edicts (30:55)

  - Force each player or opponent to sacrifice, usually creatures, and the controller chooses which.
  - Less precise but cheap and easy to repeat.

| Card | Note |
| --- | --- |

| Soul Shatter | Hits highest mana value creature or planeswalker |
| Torment of Hailfire | Often ends the game at X of 10+ |
| Grave Pact | Repeatable; discourages opponents from deploying |
| Liliana, Dreadhorde General | \-4 makes each player sacrifice two; also draws cards |
| Grenzo-style Wrinkle, Master of Pranks | Discard and sacrifice modes on combat damage (as named in captions) |
