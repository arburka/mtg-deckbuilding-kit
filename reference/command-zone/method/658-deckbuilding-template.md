# Commander Deckbuilding Template for the New Era (The Command Zone #658)

  - **Channel:** The Command Zone (hosts Rachel Weeks and Josh Lee Kwai)
  - **Published:** Feb 18, 2025
  - **Length:** 1:46:35
  - **Views:** about 693K
  - **Source:** the video's auto-generated captions, summarized below

## Purpose of the template

  - It gets a new deck to a working 100 cards so you can start playing and then adjust.
  - The numbers are averages and guidelines, not rules. Few decks will match every number.
  - Its main job is to make sure you include the "vegetables" (lands, draw, ramp, interaction) that are easy to cut while brewing.
  - The episode does not cover cEDH, which plays to much faster clocks.

## The core numbers

| Category | Count | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lands | 38 | MDFCs count as lands; landcyclers can arguably count |
| Card advantage | 12+ | One card that nets you access to more than one card |
| Ramp | 10 (up to 12) | Ongoing mana beyond your normal land drops |
| Targeted disruption | 12 | One card deals with one opposing card |
| Mass disruption | 6 | One card deals with many opposing cards |
| Plan cards | \\~30 | Enablers, payoffs, enhancers |

The categories add up to 108 cards. Cards that fill more than one role close the gap down to 99.

## Lands (9:51)

  - Recommendations across the community range from about 30 to 45 lands. Frank Karsten's math gives about 43, which the hosts call very high for Commander.
  - With 38 lands, the odds in a 7-card opening hand are:

      - about 55% to have 3 or more lands
      - about 83% to have 2 or more lands
      - about 66% to have 3 lands within the top 8 cards
  - Most players drift below their target number, so start at 38 and resist cutting lands.
  - Keeping a two-land hand makes it harder to hit your 4th, 5th, and 6th land drops later.
  - The more specific your opening-hand needs are, the more lands you should run.
  - Play MDFCs as lands first. Use them as spells only when you are flooded.
  - Deeper dive: Episode 455, "The Ultimate Guide to Lands."

## Card advantage (21:18)

  - These cards give you net extra cards. Examples:

      - Draw spells and repeatable draw engines
      - Impulse draw (exiling cards you can play for a limited time)
      - Land Tax
      - Self-mill, but only in decks that can reliably play cards from the graveyard
  - Not card advantage:

      - Ponder and Faithless Looting, which only improve card selection
      - Mindstone and Sensei's Divining Top, which only replace themselves
      - Teferi's Ageless Insight-style effects, unless the deck already draws heavily
      - Sylvan Library, unless you pay the life
  - Run at least 12, and 15 or more if you can. Extra draw makes up for having fewer lands or less removal.
  - The weaker your individual cards are, the more card draw you need.

## Ramp (36:30)

  - These cards put you ahead of your normal mana progression. Examples are mana dorks, Rampant Growth-style spells, and Arcane Signet.
  - Rituals such as Dark Ritual and Jeska's Will are not counted. They give a one-time burst rather than a lasting advantage.
  - Probability of having at least one ramp card:

| Ramp count | In opening hand | By turn 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 54% | 63% |
| 12 | — | 70.4% |

  - Hitting your land drops matters more than ramp. If you miss land drops, your ramp only catches you up.
  - Run more 2-mana ramp with a 4-mana commander. Run less with a 2- or 3-mana commander.
  - Deeper dive: Episode 303, "Complete Guide to Mana Ramp."

## Targeted disruption (45:54)

  - This category replaces the old "removal" label. It covers:

      - Kill, bounce, phase-out, and tap effects
      - Counterspells
      - Land destruction such as Wasteland
      - Targeted graveyard hate such as Scavenging Ooze
  - Protection spells like Heroic Intervention don't count. They protect your plan rather than disrupting an opponent's.
  - Run 12, which gives you about 70% odds of holding one by turn 2.
  - Holding interaction affects how you sequence your plays even when you don't cast it.

## Mass disruption (53:20)

  - This category expands the old "board wipes" label. It includes:

      - Wraths and Bane of Progress
      - Stax-style pieces such as Propaganda, Collector Ouphe, and Stony Silence
      - Fogs and Teferi's Protection
      - Graveyard wipes such as Bojuka Bog
      - Decimate and Grasp of Fate
  - Rule of thumb: if the play leaves you at card parity or better against the table, count it as mass disruption. Otherwise count it as targeted.
  - Overload cards like Vandal Blast and Cyclonic Rift go in the category you usually use them for. Avoid counting them in both.
  - Run 6, spread across threats:

      - 2–3 creature wipes
      - About 1 artifact/enchantment answer
      - About 1 graveyard answer
  - Shift the mix based on your deck's weaknesses.

## Categorizing cards (1:02:34)

  - Tag every card by category in a deckbuilding tool. The hosts use Archidekt and tag by hand.
  - Tagging shows where the deck is short and which cards fill more than one role.
  - Examples of cards that count twice:

      - Sink into Stupor: land and disruption
      - Ghost Quarter: land and disruption
      - Reclamation Sage in an Elves deck: disruption and tribal plan card
      - Insidious Fungus: ramp and disruption

## Plan cards (1:08:21)

Three subtypes:

  - **Enablers** are cards the plan cannot function without. In Aristocrats, these are token makers and sac outlets. Protection and recursion also count here.
  - **Payoffs** reward you for executing the plan. Examples are Blood Artist and enchantresses. Win conditions like Craterhoof Behemoth usually go here.
  - **Enhancers** multiply the plan but do nothing alone. Examples are Doubling Season and Teysa Karlov. Limit these, because decks tend to overload on them.

What the commander does shapes the rest of the 99:

| Commander role | Example | How to build around it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payoff | Yuriko | Load up on enablers |
| Enabler | Kaalia | Add protection and haste; somewhat more payoffs |
| Enhancer | Isshin | As many enablers as possible; few extra multipliers |

As a check, you should be able to describe your deck's plan in one sentence.

## Mana curve (1:22:14)

Rachel's averages across her decks, with outliers removed:

| Mana value | Avg per deck | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.22 | Rare in casual play |
| 1 | 9 | Mostly ramp and targeted removal |
| 2 | 18.5 | Highest slot; most ramp lands here |
| 3 | 13.9 | Engine pieces; big drop from 2 |
| 4 | 10.5 | Pairs well with 2-mana ramp |
| 5 | 4.4 | Awkward slot; mostly flexible instants like Mystic Confluence |
| 6 | 3.1 | Modal board wipes and finishers |
| 7 | 1.65 | Game-ending or high-value cards |
| 8+ | 1.27 | Includes Blasphemous Act and split cards |

  - **Simplified curve template:** 9 one-drops, 18 two-drops, 15 three-drops, 10 four-drops, 5 five-drops, 5 six-plus, plus 38 lands and 1 commander.
  - **Why five- and six-drops are rare:** board wipes cluster around turns 5–6, so the hosts avoid spending a whole turn on one big permanent then.
  - **Tools:** Archidekt's stats tab shows your curve.
  - **Deeper dive:** Episode 453, "Mastering Your Mana Curve."

## Closing guidance (1:38:25)

  - Build to the numbers first. Adjust only after goldfishing and real games show you a problem, such as missed land drops, too few sac outlets, or getting run over.
  - A follow-up episode covers when and how to deviate from the template ("The Problem with Deckbuilding Templates," #659).
  - The main takeaway, repeated several times: run 38 lands.
