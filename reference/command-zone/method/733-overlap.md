## Purpose and overview

  - Defines **overlap**: cards that fill more than one role in a deck, which adds synergy and consistency.
  - Explains why the hosts' Commander deck building template adds up to 108 cards instead of 100.
  - Walks through categories of overlap: MDFCs, modal spells, split spells, multi-use permanents, type-relevant spells, tutors, and commanders.
  - Covers the downsides: added complexity, and the risk of greedy play with flexible cards.
  - Ends with a deck building technique for tracking overlap with categories in Archidekt, and each host's favorite overlap cards.

## 6:18 Why is Overlap Important

  - **More choices per hand:** a seven-card hand where several cards have more than one mode plays as if it held extra spells. Examples given are an MDFC that can be a spell or a land, and a spree card that can be protection or card draw.
  - **Consistency:** a higher density of good effects means you draw them more often.
  - **Synergy:** overlap is described as another way of saying synergy. It includes card types and interactions, not just modal text.
  - **Answer to "good stuff" criticism:** a card that is strong in one strategy can be weak in another, so "good stuff" differs from deck to deck.
  - **Decision-making is fun:** choices are what make a turn engaging. Without them, the hosts joke, an AI could play your turn.
  - **Frees up slots:** a card that is both a land and disruption condenses two slots into one, leaving room for pet cards.

## 12:05 Overlap Increases Complexity

  - More overlap means bigger decision trees and more text on each card.
  - Use less overlap when building for a new player or for anyone who wants a simpler deck.
  - Players who only get one night a month to play may prefer straightforward decks.

## 13:28 MDFCs

MDFCs (modal double-faced cards) usually have a spell on the front and a land on the back, so they mostly replace lands.

| Card | Roles counted | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sink into Stupor | Targeted disruption, land | Described as an expensive uncommon |
| Sundering Eruption | Targeted disruption, mass disruption, land | Can destroy a land or clear blockers |
| Birgi, God of Storytelling // Harnfel, Horn of Bounty | Ramp or card draw | Only count it as both if you would realistically cast either side |
| Tergrid | Front side only | Example of an MDFC whose back face is not worth counting |

  - MDFCs are single-use: you get one side or the other unless you have recursion.
  - **Warning:** don't hold an MDFC as a spell and miss a land drop. Early mana matters more. If you usually cast Sink into Stupor as removal, don't count it toward your land total.
  - MDFCs are costly and harder for Wizards to reprint.

## 18:32 Modal Spells

| Card | Modes and roles |
| --- | --- |
| Rakdos Charm | Artifact removal, graveyard exile at instant speed, or a win condition (each creature deals 1 damage to its controller) |
| Mystic Confluence | Choose three modes, repeats allowed: counter unless the caster pays 3, bounce, or draw. Counted as targeted disruption, card advantage, and protection |
| Bushwhack | Fetch a basic land or fight. Counted as a land, because 1 mana for a land plays like a tapped basic |
| Traverse the Ulvenwald | Fetch a land, or a creature with delirium |
| Lórien Revealed | Draw three, or islandcycling for 1. Card draw and land, plus instant/sorcery, cycling, and search triggers |
| Generous Ent | Lord of the Rings landcycler the hosts would play mainly because it is also a land |
| Troll of Khazad-dûm | Swampcycler; also hard to block |
| Glacierwood Siege | Choose Temur (self-mill) or Sultai (play lands from the graveyard). Both are plan roles |

  - **Rule of thumb:** a search effect that costs 1 mana can count as a land. At 2 mana it becomes questionable.
  - Overlap can be entirely within the plan category. Self-mill plus land recursion still counts.
  - A modal permanent only counts as overlap if you would actually choose either mode.
  - One-mana landcyclers also see play in Canadian Highlander, a competitive format.

## 30:32 Split Spells

Split spells put two spells on one card. Both halves can often be used, which is different from an MDFC.

| Card | Roles |
| --- | --- |
| Virtue of Persistence | 2-mana removal (adventure side) plus a 7-mana recursion enchantment |
| Monk Class | Cost reduction (ramp), bounce a nonland permanent (targeted disruption), then casting from the top of the library (card advantage). Levels must be unlocked in order |
| Dollmaker's Shop // Porcelain Gallery | A toy-token engine plus a large anthem. Either room can be unlocked first |

  - Some halves are weaker versions of an effect, such as sorcery-speed removal. Jimmy counts Virtue of Persistence as roughly half a removal spell.
  - Only play these if you want every half. Don't include a card for modes you never plan to use.
  - Value depends on the playgroup. Sink into Stupor, for example, is stronger in some metagames than in others.

## 43:53 Tutors

  - Tutors are the ultimate overlap, because a Demonic Tutor or Vampiric Tutor can stand in for any card in the deck.
  - Count a tutor as whatever it will most often find, such as Sol Ring for ramp or a combo piece.
  - **Narrow tutors** are only as flexible as their targets. Examples:

      - With Enlightened Tutor, include targets like Ghostly Prison and Grasp of Fate.
      - With Worldly Tutor, include creature answers like Reclamation Sage and graveyard hate.
  - The hosts suggest tutors could be played more for consistency. At high power levels, though, they shift toward simply winning the game.
  - Think of tutors as a toolbox.

## 47:18 Commanders with Overlap

Strong modern commanders often combine plan with card advantage or ramp.

| Commander | Overlap |
| --- | --- |
| Esper commander (captions: "Ishola, Knight-blessed") | Draws when a player lost 4+ life, and its noncreature-spell trigger causes that life loss |
| Pantlaza, Sun-Favored | Discover gives card advantage and cheats on mana |
| Commander the captions call "Balin" | Tapping tokens produces mana, card draw, or +1/+1 counters and trample |
| Arcades, the Strategist | Draws on defender creatures and turns them into attackers. Very directional, so most builds look alike |

  - A commander with overlap tends to be powerful because it asks little of the deck.
  - Overlap is a tool, not a mandate. The template only expects about eight overlapping cards.
  - Type and theme overlap, such as building disruption into a typal deck, gives a deck identity beyond generic good stuff.

## Closing guidance

  - Overlap adds consistency, flexibility, and decision-making to a deck.
  - Wizards is printing more multi-ability cards, so take advantage of them.
  - Weigh overlap against complexity and keep playing the fundamentals, such as making land drops.
  - Not every card needs to overlap. If a deck underperforms, too little overlap may be the cause.
