## What Makes a Good Beginner Commander (2:06)

  - **One or two colors**

      - The hardest part for new players is the number of decisions they face.
      - Fewer colors means they can play almost any land in hand.
      - It also means fewer sequencing mistakes and simpler mana math.
      - Rachel's first deck was three colors, and she couldn't cast Necropotence.
  - **Clear strategy on the card**

      - Ghalta is cited as a counterexample: a strong card, but its text doesn't tell you what the deck should do.
      - A commander whose text describes the plan makes the other 99 cards easier to understand, for the player and for the table.
  - **Value engine at a reasonable power level**

      - Rachel saw a new player's deck led by Jodah, which was removed on the first turn.
      - Jodah is a must-answer threat. Its "invisible text" (a global +X/+X boost you have to track in your head) also makes it hard for beginners.
  - **Room to upgrade**

      - Owning a deck includes customizing it and finding new cards for it, for example at prereleases.
  - **They like it**

      - Ask simple questions: big things or many things, cute or burly.
      - Ask about roles in other games, such as tank, healer, or mage in MMOs.
  - Most players still own some version of their first deck.

## Clear Strategy (30:59)

  - A clear strategy answers two questions: what the commander wants, and how it wins the game.

### Typal

| Commander | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ureni of the Unwritten | Dragons | 7 mana, 7/7. Finds a dragon from the top 8 cards when it enters or attacks. Simpler than other five-color dragon commanders: half ramp, half dragons. |
| Gishath, Sun's Avatar | Dinosaurs | 8 mana. Has haste. Combat damage reveals cards and puts all dinosaurs onto the battlefield. |
| Edgar Markov | Vampires | Eminence: makes vampire tokens from the command zone. Recently reprinted, but still pricey. |
| Marrow-Gnawer | Rats | Similar to Krenko. Sacrifice is self-contained in the commander. |

  - Challenges with typal decks:

      - Some types get few new cards (rats, dinosaurs). Dragons get new cards often.
      - The decks can feel repetitive.
      - The player has to genuinely like the type.

## Where Do I Start? (57:32)

  - **Start with a precon**, preferably a recent one.

      - Several commanders in the episode were precon or Secret Lair commanders: Ellivere, Valgavoth, Kaalia, Ureni, Rin and Seri, Giada.
  - **Ask local game store staff** which precons suit beginners. Precons vary a lot in complexity.
  - **Look at your own gaming history**, for example RPGs or Slay the Spire, to identify the play style you enjoy.
  - **When teaching someone, offer vibe pairs instead of colors:**

      - Wizards and faeries
      - Angels and humans
      - Demons and vampires
      - Dragons and goblins
  - **Start from a card that already excited them**, such as one they saw in a video or opened in a pack.

## Closing Guidance

  - Choose a commander that checks a few boxes: one or two colors, a plan written on the card, a self-contained value engine, reasonable power, and room to grow.
  - Most important of all, the player has to like it.
  - Strong commanders such as Krenko or Edgar are fine for beginners if they reduce decision-making.
  - Creature combat is the clearest path for new players.
  - Start with a recent precon if you are unsure.
