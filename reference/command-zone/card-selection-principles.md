# Card Selection Principles — why a pick stands out

Distilled from The Command Zone's card-list episodes (drafts, "10 for $10", awards) and card-evaluation episodes. The lists themselves age quickly. The reasoning behind them is what's kept here: what made the hosts pick a card over the obvious, most-played option.

> Card names are the hosts' examples, as described in the episodes. **Verify oracle text and price via Scryfall before using any of them in a deck.**

Episode keys used below: **Rocks** (Best Mana Rocks 3+), **704** (10 Best Colorless for $10), **Lands** (10 for $10: Utility Lands), **Disr** (10 for $10: Disruption), **619** (Best "Gotcha" Spells), **Sorc** (Best Big Splashy Sorceries), **Zony** (Zony Awards 2025), **2024** (2024 staff picks), **682** (Staples Worth It?), **Cheap** (Cheap Replacements for Staples), **744** (Cards We Were Wrong About).

---

## 1. Earn the slot twice

The strongest picks do their category's job *and* something else, so they fill two template slots at once (see `method/733-overlap.md`).

- **A rock costing 3+ must justify itself** by drawing, removing something, or producing more than one mana. Each of the three consensus top rocks did something besides make mana. *(Rocks)*
- **Disruption that also draws, ramps, or protects** never feels dead: a kill spell whose convoking creatures loot (Lethal Scheme), a 1-drop that ramps early or kills an artifact late (Insidious Fungus), a removal spell that also protects your commander (Collective Resistance). *(Disr)*
- **Utility lands turn a land slot into a spell slot**, which makes running a full 38 lands cheaper. *(Lands)*
- **One card replacing what used to take three** (ramp + card advantage + a body) is what wins "best creature." *(Zony)*

## 2. Effective cost beats printed cost

- **The best big spells cost far less than their mana value** in practice (Blasphemous Act, Treasure Cruise, Afterlife from the Loam). *(Sorc)*
- **Count the full activation cost.** Access Tunnel's effective cost is 4 vs. Rogue's Passage's 5, and Rogue's Passage often never gets activated. A cheap, usable effect beats an expensive "finisher." *(Lands)*
- **Net cost on the turn it lands.** A 4-mana rock that taps for 3 immediately costs about 1. *(Rocks)*
- **A very expensive activation is a warning sign**: if using it takes ~8 mana, you are usually already losing. *(Zony)*
- **Alternative costs (convoke, delve) let you cast with no open mana** or far below sticker price. *(619, Sorc)*

## 3. Stays good at every stage of the game

- **Modal cards stay useful whether you're ahead or behind** (Farewell, the Seasons cycle, Mystic Confluence). *(Sorc, Disr)*
- **Several narrow modes add up to flexibility** when each one works at a different stage of the game. Dismissing narrow modes was a recurring misjudgment. *(744)*
- **Ask when the card is bad.** Glittering Stockpile is weak drawn late; Rampant Growth-style ramp is weak late, which is why Insidious Fungus gets the nod. *(Rocks, Disr)*
- **Instant-speed threats give value in every phase** (Wan Shi Tong's win was credited to this). *(Zony)*
- **Expensive and slow is worse at faster tables.** Rocks that enter tapped or cost 5+ rarely pay back; 6-drop sorceries are less castable. If a card costs a lot, it should have very high impact (the hosts preferred 7–8 MV haymakers over 6-drops). *(Rocks, Sorc)*

## 4. Breadth vs. ceiling

- **Decide what "best" means**: peak power, average power, breadth of use, or personal favorite. *(Zony)*
- **Breadth is its own kind of strength.** One host estimated Badgermole Cub fits ~12 of his 30 decks vs. 2–3 for the award winner. Colorless cards fit every deck. *(Zony, 704)*
- **Narrow is fine when your deck supplies what the card needs.** Most rocks past the top picks need untap effects, ETB reuse, recursion, or artifact support. That's a dependency check, not a reason to dismiss them (SOP §4). *(Rocks)*
- **Graveyard interaction widens a card's reach.** Cyclers, surveil, and recursion engines fit more decks than they first appear to. *(744)*
- **Three-color cards fit too few decks** to be general picks. *(Sorc)*

## 5. Look past the obvious use

- **Look beyond the headline mechanic.** For Pentad Prism, Noble's Purse, and Tome of Legends the hosts said proliferate is not the main reason to play them. *(704)*
- **Re-check cards that commonly get cut**; some of them overperform. *(704)*
- **Count the outputs.** A card that leaves four artifacts behind (the Purse plus three Treasures) feeds sac outlets, token payoffs, and affinity. *(704)*
- **Build the deck to make a card better.** Running only a few creatures of one type turns Heirloom Blade into a near-tutor for them. *(704)*
- **Prefer the card that fixes your deck's specific weakness** (e.g., Graaz solving tokens that enter too small to matter). *(704)*

## 6. Rules details that make a card better than its comparison card

- **Doesn't target** → works through hexproof, shroud, and protection. *(Disr)*
- **Sacrifice/edict effects** get past indestructible and hexproof. *(Disr)*
- **Bounce** beats death triggers and Heroic Intervention-style protection, and handles artifacts and enchantments. *(Sorc)*
- **Exile vs. destroy, split second, can't be countered** each change how answerable a card is. *(Sorc, 619)*
- **One answer per opponent** (Dismantling Wave, Grasp of Fate, Sheoldred's Edict) gets multiplayer value from one card. *(Disr)*
- **Scales with opponents**: triggers once per player attacked (Horn of the Mark) or untaps on every player's turn (Sting) is worth far more in a four-player pod. *(704)*
- **Read the precise text.** Mentally rounding a card to a familiar one was the hosts' biggest source of misjudgment. *(744)*

## 7. Table dynamics

- **Deterrence is value.** Open mana for a damage-prevention effect, a free chump blocker, or a held-up activation pushes attackers toward other players. *(Disr, Lands, 704)*
- **Political cards let opponents fight each other** (a removal effect where the damaged player chooses the target; vote cards that help the table gang up on the leader). They're risky if you're already the leader. *(704, Disr)*
- **Scales to the table's power** when it uses opponents' resources (mass reanimation from every graveyard), which fits lower brackets better than flat game-enders. *(Sorc)*
- **Mind the feel-bad factor.** Theft draws the strongest complaints; some cards make games feel unwinnable; some (Basalt Monolith) signal combo to the table. *(Sorc, 619, Rocks)* See SOP §4, "Reject feel-bad cards."
- **Two or three "gotcha" cards per deck.** The surprise matters: the opponent's own play backfires. A reputation for tricks makes open mana threatening even when you have nothing. *(619)*

## 8. Choosing a pick over the ubiquitous staple

- **Staples lose their surprise.** The gotcha draft deliberately excluded the most-played cards (Teferi's Protection, Deflecting Swat, Orcish Bowmasters), because everyone expects them. *(619)*
- **Pick what your colors lack.** Dismantling Wave over Generous Gift, because white already has plenty of efficient removal. *(Disr)*
- **Judge speed by your deck's play pattern.** A sorcery-speed answer was preferred over instant alternatives in red, where impulse draw and aggressive starts make speed matter less. *(Disr)*
- **Popular doesn't mean best.** The hosts disagreed with several award winners (e.g., a two-mana fixer that draws late over an eight-mana-effective artifact; an equipment called overrated for lacking trample or protection). *(Zony)*
- **Money buys consistency, efficiency, flexibility, and scarcity, not synergy.** Ten synergistic cards improve a deck more than one outlier you may never draw. Narrow, strategy-specific cards are cheap and strong. *(682)*
- **Redundancy and consistency over explosive doublers or protection pieces.** *(Cheap)*
- **Know why you're buying a card; try the cheap alternative first.** *(682)*

## 9. Evaluation traps *(744)*

| Pattern | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Treating a card as the same as its comparison card | Identify comparable cards, then weigh every difference |
| Undervaluing damage and life payment | Life totals matter more as games get faster |
| Undervaluing the graveyard | Any graveyard interaction makes a card fit in more decks |
| Dismissing narrow modes | Several narrow modes that each work at different stages add up to flexibility |
| Mentally rounding to a familiar card | Read the precise text |

## Checklist: before locking a candidate

1. **What does it do besides its main job?** (§1)
2. **What does it actually cost in play** — effective cost, net cost the turn it lands? (§2)
3. **When in the game is it bad?** (§3)
4. **Does this deck supply what it needs?** (§4, SOP §4 dependency check)
5. **What rules detail makes it better or worse than its comparison card?** Read the exact text. (§6, §9)
6. **How will the table react to it?** (§7)
7. **Why this over the staple?** (§8)
