## Purpose / Overview

  - Looks at expensive Commander staples and asks whether each one is worth its price, not just whether it is powerful.

  - Offers cheaper alternatives that cover part or all of each card's function.
  - Splits the list into general staples, cards that may be too strong for casual groups, and Game Changers that only fit bracket 3 and up.
  - Ends with a discussion of what money actually buys in Magic and how to spend it.

## Fetchlands (0:01:48)

  - Covers the 10 traditional fetchlands (pay 1 life, search for one of two basic land types).
  - Prices range from about $14 to $33; they once sat above $50, and Khans of Tarkir reprints dropped them to $10-15.
  - Demand comes from multiple formats (Modern, Legacy), not just Commander.
  - Mechanical value: strong in sacrifice, graveyard, and landfall decks.
  - Cheaper partial substitutes: Evolving Wilds, Terramorphic Expanse, Prismatic Vista ($40, basics only).
  - Fetchlands are only as good as what they fetch: shocks, triomes, surveil lands, tango lands, and cycling lands.
  - Off-color fetches are mostly for landfall decks; running out of fetch targets is a real risk.
  - A functional mana base is possible without them, as most precons show, but fetches turn the mana base into part of the deck's engine.
  - More competitive pods see more benefit from the small, cumulative edge.
  - Verdict: worth it at some point; they retain value and are reprinted regularly, so timing matters.

## Channel Lands (0:13:07)

  - Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty lands with discard-to-activate abilities, cheaper per legendary creature you control.
  - Prices: Boseiju, Who Endures $45; Otawara, Soaring City $28; Takenuma $11; Eiganjo $6.50; Sokenzan cheaper still.
  - Boseiju is described as winning or stopping games more than any other land; Otawara is strong against silence effects.
  - Alternatives: MDFCs, Final Fantasy adventure lands, Sink into Stupor (about $10), and Lord of the Rings landcyclers such as Troll of Khazad-dum.
  - Analogy: you are paying for the extra spell slot, not the effect itself.
  - Downside: they may only improve a deck marginally.
  - Friends use one copy across decks with proxy markers or a binder of expensive cards.
  - Verdict: worth it; they have only one printing and are due, but the reprint queue is long.

## Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx (0:21:29)

  - $50; taps for mana equal to devotion to a chosen color.
  - Strongest in mono-color and heavy single-color decks; gives white and red explosive acceleration; weakest in black.
  - No good land substitute outside Cabal Coffers and narrow black options.
  - Printed only in Theros, The List, and a Pro Tour promo.
  - Verdict: do not buy now; a reprint would likely drop it to about $20.

  - Alternatives:

## Heroic Intervention (0:39:22)

  - $14 despite many Universes Beyond reprints.
  - Alternatives: Rapid Invigoration-style regeneration (Ram Through-era Rapid Vigor named as about $2), Smuggler's Surprise (about $9), Collective Resistance, Snakeskin Veil, Overprotect.
  - Verdict: still worth it, but expected to be reprinted within a year; often acquired through precons.

## The Great Henge (0:46:33)

  - $80.
  - Alternatives: Guardian Project ($14), Beast Whisperer (under $5), Garruk's Uprising (75 cents), Tribute to the World Tree ($15).
  - More of a build-around than it looks; needs creatures with high power.
  - Verdict: not worth it.

## The Ozolith (0:48:54)

  - $40; stores counters from creatures that leave and moves them to a new creature at combat.
  - Only helps after you have already been blown out, and moves all counters at once, which is telegraphed.
  - Better suited to sacrifice or modular-style decks.
  - Verdict: not worth $40; Heroic Intervention or Snakeskin Veil protect boards more directly.

## Sword of Feast and Famine (0:52:12)

  - $28; untaps lands on combat damage and gives protection from black and green.
  - Alternatives:

| Card | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Bear Umbra | $10 |
| Nature's Will | $3.50 (was $20-40 before reprint) |
| Reset | About $6 after Mystery Booster 2 |
| Wilderness Reclamation | $0.75 |

  - Cheaper untap effects are all green or blue; for black or white, the sword may be worth it.
  - Verdict: situational; best in equipment decks or mana-hungry decks.

## Roaming Throne (0:56:01)

  - $40; doubles triggers for a chosen creature type, has ward 2, colorless.
  - Few close substitutes; Spark Double, Irenicus's Vile Duplication, Harmonic Prodigy for wizards, and Annie Joins Up noted as partial options.
  - Ward 2 may make reprints less likely.
  - Verdict: worth it, especially for typal decks.

## How Does Playgroup Change Things? (0:58:44)

  - Remaining cards are power outliers for average pods.
  - Buying them can backfire if the group stops wanting to play against them.

## Mana Drain (0:59:31)

  - $50 (previously about $100); counterspell plus delayed mana burst.
  - Alternatives: Counterspell, Delay, Turnabout, Dramatic Reversal (all under $10).
  - Rachel only runs it at high bracket 3 or bracket 4.
  - Verdict: worth it only if appropriate for the playgroup.

## Esper Sentinel (1:02:19)

  - $43; strongest white card-draw engine for its cost.
  - Alternatives: Archivist of Oghma, Mask of Memory, Welcoming Vampire, Tymna, Wedding Ring, Smuggler's Share, Mangara, the monarch.
  - Groups that know how to play around Rhystic Study-style effects will simply pay the tax.
  - Verdict: for competitive setups only; otherwise shop around.

## How Does the Game Changer List Change Things? (1:06:05)

  - Remaining cards are Game Changers; skip them unless playing bracket 3 or higher.

## Cyclonic Rift (1:06:19)

  - $48 (once $3-5); instant-speed one-sided bounce of all non-land permanents when overloaded.
  - Alternatives: Rivers' Rebuke, Desynchronization (Assassin's Creed), Perplexing Test, Crux of Fate-style one-sided wipes.
  - Verdict: the best of its kind, but wait for a reprint (typically $20-25).

## Teferi's Protection (1:10:20)

  - $50; three-mana white instant that protects life total and board.
  - Alternatives: Flare of Fortitude, Angel's Grace, Clever Concealment, Everybody Lives.
  - Lets a lower-powered deck compete with stronger decks at the table.
  - Verdict: worth it for bracket 3+; hold mana open for it from about turn six on.

## Field of the Dead (1:13:39)

  - $55; makes zombies on landfall once you control seven or more differently named lands.
  - No true replacement as a land, but it is narrower than it looks and requires a built-around mana base.
  - Last printed in M20 as a common, plus special versions.
  - Verdict: not worth it; wait for a reprint even for a dedicated lands deck.

## What Does Money Buy? (1:16:28)

  - Price tends to rise as mana value falls, as long as the card is good.
  - Money buys consistency, efficiency, flexibility, and scarcity.
  - Efficiency matters less in Commander than in Modern, since modal cards can fill those slots.
  - Money does not need to buy synergy or power: ten synergistic cards improve a deck more than one outlier you may never draw.
  - Narrow cards built for specific strategies are cheap and strong (for example, Perplexing Test, Crux of Fate).
  - Jimmy's example: his first expensive buy was Strip Mine ($15), when Ghost Quarter, Demolition Field, or Field of Ruin would have done the job for about 50 cents.
  - Other ways to spend: alters, foils, artist proofs.
  - Warning against burnout from buying power that makes games less fun.

## Closing Guidance

  - Worth buying: fetchlands (time the reprint), channel lands, Roaming Throne, Akroma's Will, Heroic Intervention, and Teferi's Protection for bracket 3+.
  - Not worth buying now: Nykthos, Shadowspear, Cyclonic Rift (wait for reprints), white token doublers, The Great Henge, The Ozolith, Field of the Dead.
  - Depends on playgroup: Mana Drain, Esper Sentinel, Sword of Feast and Famine.
  - Know why you are buying a card; if unsure, try the cheap alternative first.
