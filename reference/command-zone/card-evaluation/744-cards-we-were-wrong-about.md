## Purpose / Overview

  - Looks back at set reviews from Brothers' War onward, which is when Rachel joined the show.
  - Covers three kinds of misses: cards that were overrated, cards that were underrated, and cards that never came up in a review.
  - For each card, compares the original evaluation with how the card actually plays in Commander today.
  - Treats the exercise as a way to recalibrate, by working out why each miss happened so the same mistake is less likely next time.
  - Ends with general lessons on how the hosts tend to misjudge cards.

## Intro (0:00)

  - Rachel and Josh introduce the topic: cards they got wrong in past set reviews.
  - Revisiting misses builds evaluation skill. Understanding why a card was misjudged makes it easier to spot the same error later.

## Loran of the Third Path (2:14)

  - **Card:** 2-mana white 2/1 with vigilance. On ETB it destroys up to one artifact or enchantment. It also has a tap ability that makes you and a target opponent each draw a card.
  - **Original take:** Seen as Reclamation Sage in white, with the draw ability rarely used.
  - **Reality:** Rachel now runs Loran in about three decks and Reclamation Sage in none.
  - **Why it overperforms:**

      - The draw ability gets used often. It supports politics, and it can help the table catch up to a runaway leader.
      - It fits legendary, blink, political, and activated-ability decks.
      - White has blink effects and recursion for creatures with mana value 3 or less, which pairs well with Sun Titan and reanimator or lifegain shells.
      - Card draw is scarcer in white, so the ability is worth more there.
      - Meanwhile, Reclamation Sage lost ground to cheaper options like Haywire Mite.
  - **Lesson:** An extra line of text that seems minor can push a card well past its comparison card.

## Breach the Multiverse (6:11)

  - **Card:** 7-mana black sorcery. Each player mills 10, then you take a creature or planeswalker from each graveyard.
  - **Original take (episode 529, Josh and Jimmy):** Compared to Command the Dread Horde. The hosts said it would need to be the first or second best effect of its type to be worth playing, and they focused on graveyard synergy.
  - **What was missed:**

      - It is self-contained. The mill 10 sets up its own targets, so it works as a strong big spell in any deck.
      - Against a deck with about 30 creatures, 10 milled cards typically show 3 or 4 to choose from.
      - Late in a game, it can return about 25 mana worth of permanents.
      - Opponents need a counterspell or a board wipe to deal with it, since spot removal is not enough.
      - Faster games make paying life more costly, so one extra mana beats paying about 20 life for Command the Dread Horde.
      - Forking or recasting it is even stronger, because the second cast has 20 milled cards to choose from.
  - **Caveat raised at the time:** It works against Living Death, Exhume, and Incarnation Technique, because it puts creatures into opponents' graveyards. The hosts now say this concern was given too much weight.
  - **Deeper dive:** The Command Zone 529, The Best Cards (In the 99) for March of the Machine.

## Etali, Primal Conqueror (11:49)

  - **Original take (episode 533):** Called Josh's worst evaluation. When asked for the strongest compleated commanders, Jimmy chose Heliod, the Radiant Dawn and Josh chose Rona, Herald of Invasion.
  - **What was missed:**

      - The hosts focused on building synergy around Etali, such as blink and cast-from-exile effects. In practice, the deck just copies the ETB as many times as possible: all ramp plus Twinflame-style effects.
      - The trigger cannot whiff. The original Etali misses about 30% of the time.
      - It triggers on ETB rather than on attack, so it can go off several times in one turn.
      - The name made the hosts mentally equate it with the original Etali.
      - The flip side, which plays like Blightsteel Colossus, distracted from the front. Players rarely want to transform it.
  - **Deeper dive:** The Command Zone 533, Compleated Commanders for March of the Machine.

## Lord of the Rings Cyclers (19:59)

  - **Cards:** Lórien Revealed, Troll of Khazad-dûm, Generous Ent, Oliphaunt, Eagles of the North. Each has land-type cycling for 1.
  - **Background:** Never covered in a set review. At release, Rachel tweeted that they play like tapped MDFCs, though she counted them as spells.
  - **Current usage:**

| Card | Rachel | Josh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lórien Revealed | Every copy she owns is in a deck | Rates highly |
| Troll of Khazad-dûm | In decks | Rates highly |
| Generous Ent | 2 decks | Not used |
| Eagles of the North | 2 decks | Not used |
| Oliphaunt | Not used | Not used |

  - **Why they work:**

      - They can fetch typed nonbasic lands, including Mystic Sanctuary and the Triomes.
      - They put a card in the graveyard. That helps reanimator, delirium, and card-type decks; Octavia is given as an example for Lórien Revealed.
      - Eagles of the North is in Rachel's Angel reanimator deck and her cycling/cascade deck.
  - **Cost threshold:** Cycling for 1 works like a land that enters tapped. Cycling for 2 is much worse and compares to the guildgates that require paying 1 when they enter.

## Ensnared by the Mara (26:16)

  - **Card:** 3-mana red sorcery with a villainous choice. Each opponent either gives you a free spell from the top of their library or exiles the top 4 cards and takes damage equal to their total mana value.
  - **Background:** Never covered. It has become popular in the hosts' playgroup.
  - **Points made:**

      - Opponents must choose before seeing which spell they would give up.
      - Late in the game, or against high-curve decks, taking the damage is dangerous.
      - If no one takes damage, you can get three free spells for 3 mana.
      - Rachel compares it to a much stronger Browbeat. It suits burn, copy, and pressure decks.
      - Damage also hurts decks that pay life for resources, such as decks using Black Market Connections.
      - Copying it with forking effects or Fury Storm puts opponents in a very difficult spot.

## Sergeant John Benton (30:53)

  - **Card:** 3-mana Selesnya 2/4 with trample and haste. When it deals combat damage to a player, you and that player each draw that many cards.
  - **Points made:**

      - Pump spells can make it draw 12 or more cards.
      - Opponents receive their cards only after taking the damage.
      - Green and white provide strong protection, and the refilled hand protects the commander.
      - The deck plays somewhat like an infect deck. Opponents struggle to decide whether to block when pump spells are likely.
      - It can come down as early as turn 2.

## Kutzil, Malamet Exemplar (33:07)

  - **Card:** 3-mana Selesnya 3/3. Opponents cannot cast spells during your turn. You draw a card when creatures with power above their base power deal combat damage to a player.
  - **Background:** Never covered. According to EDHREC, it is now the most-played green-white card, though EDHREC tracks only about two years.
  - **Why it was misjudged:**

      - It looked narrow, but nearly every Selesnya strategy (+1/+1 counters, anthems, equipment, tokens, auras) turns on the draw.
      - Shutting off spells on your turn protects combat and aura stacks from being blown out.
      - It reads as a 2-mana stax effect plus a bigger body and card draw for one more green mana.
      - Its similarity to Sovereign Okinec Ahau may have caused the hosts to pick only one of the two to discuss.
  - **Lesson:** Evaluate a card by what its color pair usually does.

## Rose, Cutthroat Raider (38:14)

  - **Card:** 3-mana red 3/2 artifact creature with first strike. After combat, it creates a junk token for each opponent you attacked, and it adds red mana whenever you sacrifice a junk.
  - **Background:** Missed because Fallout was covered mostly through precon episodes.
  - **Points made:**

      - Each junk works like a red Treasure that also draws a card.
      - Attacking one opponent works out to 4 mana for a card plus a red mana. Attacking two doubles that.
      - Stored junk gives some recovery after a board wipe.
      - It is good with extra combats and tokens, and first strike makes it hard to block.
      - It will improve if more junk-producing cards are printed.

## Sundering Eruption (46:00)

  - **Card:** MDFC. The front is a 3-mana red sorcery that destroys a land (its controller fetches a basic tapped) and stops creatures without flying from blocking. The back, Volcanic Fissure, can enter untapped for 3 life.
  - **Original take (episode 615):** Both Rachel and Jimmy graded it a C.
  - **Reality:**

      - Play it as a land early. Late in the game, it wins by making a board effectively unblockable against players who are holding back defenders.
      - It also answers problem lands such as Maze of Ith and Gaea's Cradle.
      - Compared to Generous Gift, it also works as a land and as a finisher.
      - Now re-graded to about A among MDFCs, below Sink into Stupor and alongside Hydroelectric Specimen and Pinnacle Monk.
      - It is recommended for any red deck, not only aggressive ones.
  - **Deeper dive:** The Command Zone 615, We Rank the MDFCs in MH3.

## Springheart Nantuko (51:26)

  - **Card:** 2-mana enchantment creature with bestow for the same cost. With landfall, pay 2 to make a token copy of the enchanted creature, or get a 1/1 insect if you don't.
  - **Background:** Never covered. Josh could not explain the omission.
  - **Examples given:**

      - Copying Etali; one fetch land produces two Etali triggers.
      - A turn-one mana creature, then turn-two bestow on it, then copying Wood Elves with each land drop.
      - Also good with Torrential Gearhulk and Snapcaster Mage.
  - **Why it is strong:**

      - It works in landfall, blink, aura, and token decks.
      - It survives board wipes and still makes insects.
      - One copy is roughly equivalent to a 4-mana clone, and three copies can decide a game.
      - Josh says it is far stronger than Young Pyromancer.

## Surveil Lands (55:39)

  - **Cards:** Commercial District, Elegant Parlor, Lush Portico, and the rest of the 10-card cycle. Each enters tapped and surveils 1.
  - **Original take:** The hosts said they were better with fetch lands and in graveyard decks.
  - **Reality:**

      - They are among the top three or five fetchable duals and below only the shock lands.
      - Rachel builds her mana bases with shock lands first, then surveil lands, and prefers them over Triomes in most decks.
      - Being fetchable lets you choose when to take the tapped land, unlike the scry lands.
      - Surveil fuels graveyard counts, card types, and creature-in-graveyard effects, which makes it better than scry.
      - Josh would include two even in decks without fetch lands if they run Nature's Lore-style ramp.
      - Rachel puts in every surveil land her colors allow, except in five-color decks.

## Blasphemous Edict (59:58)

  - **Card:** 5-mana black sorcery, or 1 mana if there are 13 or more creatures on the battlefield. Each player sacrifices 13 creatures.
  - **Original take:** Overrated. The hosts said it was worse than Toxic Deluge but the next-best black board wipe, and could go in any black deck.
  - **Reality:**

      - The opponent who is far ahead keeps their best creatures while everyone else is wiped out. After a Living Death, for example, it fails to solve the problem.
      - The threshold of 13 is much higher than Blasphemous Act's 8, so the card costs either 1 or 5, with no in-between.
      - Josh runs it in one deck and dislikes drawing it.
  - **Better black options named:** Toxic Deluge, Massacre Wurm, Game Over, Make an Example, Extinction Event, and the Spider-Man board wipe.

## Elspeth, Storm Slayer (1:04:29)

  - **Card:** 5-mana planeswalker with 5 loyalty that doubles tokens you create. Abilities: +1 makes a 1/1 soldier; 0 puts a +1/+1 counter on each creature and gives them flying until your next turn; −3 destroys an opponent's creature with mana value 3 or greater.
  - **Original take:** The hosts found it hard to evaluate and talked themselves out of it.
  - **What was missed:**

      - Because the flying lasts until your next turn, the 0 ability protects Elspeth in the same way Elspeth, Sun's Champion's minus protects her.
      - It works like Felidar Retreat every turn. Repeatedly using the 0, plus token doubling, quickly builds an overwhelming board.
      - Players untap with it much more often than the hosts expected.

## Auroral Procession (1:09:10)

  - **Card:** 2-mana Simic instant. Return any card from your graveyard to hand.
  - **Background:** Never covered.
  - **Points made:**

      - Instant speed and any card type, so it can return a counterspell, a draw spell, Torrential Gearhulk, or a land.
      - Best in interactive instant-speed decks.
      - Comparable to Peerless Recycling, which only returns permanents.
      - Josh suggests buying one copy for every blue-green deck you own.

## Afterlife from the Loam (1:11:37)

  - **Card:** 7-mana black sorcery with delve. Put one creature card from each player's graveyard onto the battlefield under your control.
  - **Background:** Never covered. It competes with Breach the Multiverse.
  - **Points made:**

      - With delve, it usually costs 3 black mana for four creatures.
      - Even a modest result is strong, such as getting a Solemn Simulacrum, a Stitcher's Supplier, and a Sakura-Tribe Elder.
      - Rachel also plays it in spellslinger decks, which fill the graveyard naturally.
      - Flashback is possible, but it costs the full 8 because delve cannot apply.

## Parapet Thrasher (1:13:29)

  - **Card:** 3-mana red 4/3 flying dragon. When dragons you control deal combat damage to an opponent, choose one mode not yet chosen this turn: destroy an artifact, deal 4 damage to each other opponent, or impulse draw.
  - **Points made:**

      - Rachel runs it in a Gogo deck. Two copies trigger each other, dealing 8 damage to each opponent and drawing two cards.
      - Strong in clone and copy decks and with myriad; Josh had it in a Game Knights deck.
      - Without dragons or copy effects, it is weaker.

## Town Cycle (1:15:24)

  - **Cards:** Midgar, Lindblum, Jidoor, Zanarkand, and Ishgard. Each is a tapped land with an adventure.
  - **Original take:** Overrated as MDFCs that let you use both halves.
  - **Reality:**

      - The adventures cost too much (3 to 6 mana), so playing both halves costs too much tempo.
      - In practice they act as mono-color tapped lands about 90% of the time.
      - Josh keeps the black one, Midgar, in a deck or two as a 3-mana sacrifice outlet that draws two. He may also run the white one.
      - Rachel's Jidoor (mill half a library for 6) is too slow; she would rather have a cycling land.

## Exploration Broodship (1:18:32)

  - **Card:** 1-mana artifact spacecraft. At 3 charge counters, you may play an extra land each turn. At 8, it becomes a 4/4 flier, and once per turn you can cast a permanent from your graveyard by also sacrificing a land.
  - **Original take:** A weaker Exploration.
  - **Reality:**

      - The graveyard recursion is the main reason to play it, and the extra land is a bonus.
      - It is better compared to Muldrotha than to Exploration.
      - Late in the game, sacrificing a land to effectively tutor a permanent onto the battlefield is well worth it.
      - Josh would include it in decks where he would not run Exploration.

## Abandoned Air Temple (1:22:40)

  - **Card:** Land that enters tapped unless you control a basic land and taps for white. An activated ability puts a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control.
  - **Original take:** Seen as similar to Gavony Township.
  - **Why it is better:**

      - It taps for colored mana, and Gavony Township does not.
      - It only needs white, so it fits Azorius and other non-green decks, while Township requires Selesnya.
      - It gives counters to colors that rarely get them; Josh cites his Shorikai deck.

## Seismic Sense (1:24:40)

  - **Card:** 2-mana green sorcery (a Lesson). Look at the top X cards, where X is the number of lands you control, and take a creature or land.
  - **Points made:**

      - Early, it is a normal green cantrip. Late, it can see 10 to 14 cards and acts close to a tutor.
      - Choosing the best of 14 cards is the key difference from Abundant Harvest.
      - It is best with 25 or more creatures and key lands such as Cabal Coffers and Urborg.

## How Do You Tend to Misevaluate Cards (1:29:38)

| Pattern | Example | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treating a card as the same as its comparison card | Abandoned Air Temple vs. Gavony Township, Loran vs. Reclamation Sage | Identify comparable cards, then weigh every difference |
| Undervaluing damage and life payment | Ensnared by the Mara, Command the Dread Horde, Black Market Connections | Life totals matter more as games get faster |
| Undervaluing the graveyard | Cyclers, surveil lands, Broodship | Any graveyard interaction makes a card fit in more decks |
| Dismissing narrow modes | Sundering Eruption | Several narrow modes that each work at different stages add up to flexibility |
| Mentally rounding to a familiar card | Etali, Elspeth's "until your next turn" | Read the precise text |

## Closing Guidance

  - Card evaluation is hard, and the hosts expect to keep missing sometimes. Opponents who use a misjudged card well are often how players learn.
  - Josh asks viewers to share their own over- and underrated cards, especially ones they bought in multiples and later shelved. He mentions several Asmoranomardicadaistinaculdacar, and Rachel mentions eight Ob Nixilis inversions.
